Today we discuss photosynthesis. Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It occurs in the chloroplasts, specifically in the thylakoid membranes for the light-dependent reactions and in the stroma for the Calvin cycle. Chlorophyll a is the primary pigment absorbing red and blue light. The overall equation is six CO2 plus six H2O plus light yields C6H12O6 plus six O2. In the light reactions, photosystem II splits water, releasing oxygen, and produces ATP via chemiosmosis and NADPH. The Calvin cycle then fixes carbon dioxide using the enzyme RuBisCO. Factors limiting the rate include light intensity, CO2 concentration and temperature.
