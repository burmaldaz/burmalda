Supply and demand is the fundamental model of a market economy. The law of demand states that as price rises the quantity demanded falls. The law of supply states that as price rises producers are willing to supply more. Market equilibrium is where supply meets demand, giving the equilibrium price and quantity. Shifts in demand can be caused by changes in income, tastes, prices of related goods, and expectations. Shifts in supply come from input prices, technology, and number of sellers. A price ceiling below equilibrium causes shortages; a price floor above equilibrium causes surpluses. Elasticity measures how much quantity responds to price.
